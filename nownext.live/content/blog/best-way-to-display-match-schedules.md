## Why Traditional Noticeboards Always Let You Down

Let's stop pretending the old methods work. Here's what actually happens:

### The moment you print it, it's wrong

Printed schedules are a snapshot in time — and time moves on fast. The first match overruns by ten minutes, and suddenly your entire schedule is fiction. A player pulls out mid-morning, and now you're doing origami with printed sheets or scribbling corrections in the margins.

By lunchtime? That clean, laminated schedule from 9am is an annotated mess of arrows, asterisks, and crossed-out names that only you can interpret — if you're lucky.

### Whiteboards become unreadable under pressure

A whiteboard sounds like the flexible solution. In practice? It's a disaster waiting to happen.

Different handwriting styles. Markers that run dry. Accidental erasures. Someone squeezes in a last-minute addition in the corner and now half the players can't tell if they're on Court 3 or Court 8. The board that started the day as a clear schedule becomes a cryptic puzzle that generates more questions than it answers.

### The "Am I on next?" spiral never ends

Here's the brutal feedback loop: the less clear your display is, the more players interrupt your volunteers to ask for updates. The more your volunteers are answering questions, the less time they have to update the board. The less updated the board is, the more players ask questions.

Your volunteers didn't volunteer to spend all day being a human FAQ. They want to *run* a great event.

### You're bleeding volunteer hours

Here's the maths no one does: if a volunteer spends 15 minutes every hour updating a whiteboard during an 8-hour event, that's **2 full hours lost** to admin. Across multiple volunteers and a full season of events, you're looking at hundreds of wasted hours every year.

Hours that could be spent welcoming new players, managing results, or just actually enjoying the event.

## What Digital Schedule Displays Do Differently

Switching to a digital display isn't just a quality-of-life improvement — it changes how your entire event *feels*.

### Every screen updates the moment you make a change

Change a court assignment on your phone. Within seconds, it's updated on the TV in the main hall, the tablet at the registration desk, and every player's own device. No printing. No rewriting. No announcement over the PA.

Players learn quickly that the display is always right. They stop asking questions. That trust alone is worth the switch.

### Crystal clear from across the room

Digital displays use consistent typography, high-contrast layouts, and large text designed to be readable from 10 metres away. No squinting. No guessing whether that's a "1" or a "7".

And crucially — it looks exactly the same at 9am as it does at 4pm. No smudging. No fading. No crossed-out chaos.

### Players spread out instead of crowding the desk

When players can check the schedule from their phone, from a screen in the café, or from a TV at the far end of the hall, they stop bottlenecking around a single noticeboard. Your venue breathes. Your registration desk stops being a queue management exercise.

### Works on whatever screens you already have

Smart TV? Laptop? Old monitor plugged into a laptop? iPad on a stand? If it has a web browser, it can show your schedule. No special hardware. No software installations. No IT department needed.

### Makes your club look like it has its act together

First impressions matter. A clean, live-updating digital display signals that you're running a professional, well-organised event. That impression sticks — with players, with visitors, and with potential sponsors.

### Do I need a smart TV?

No. Any screen with a web browser works — regular computers, laptops, tablets, smart TVs, or even old monitors connected to a Raspberry Pi. If it can open a web page, it can show your schedule.

Yes, and this is one of the best things about the platform. You can have the same live schedule on a TV in the main hall, a tablet at the desk, a laptop in the office, and every player's phone — all showing identical, real-time information. There's no limit on connected screens.

Yes — and many clubs love this feature. Share the display URL with players before or on the day and they can check what's happening next from wherever they are. It's read-only, so there's no risk of anyone accidentally editing the schedule.
