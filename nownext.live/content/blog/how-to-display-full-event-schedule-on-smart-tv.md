## The 3 Frustrating Problems with Traditional TV Schedule Displays

Before diving into the solution, let's look at why conventional methods fail during live events:

### 1. Static USB Image Drives
* **The Problem**: Exporting schedule slides to a USB thumb drive works until a speaker is late or a match finishes early.
* **Why It Fails**: Changing a single time slot requires taking down the drive, editing the file on a laptop, re-exporting, and plugging it back into the TV.

### 2. Physical Cables (HDMI from Laptops)
* **The Problem**: Running an HDMI cable from an event coordinator's laptop to a wall-mounted TV.
* **Why It Fails**: Long HDMI cables create trip hazards, and the laptop remains tethered to the screen all day, preventing staff from using it for other tasks.

### 3. Expensive Signage Media Players
* **The Problem**: Commercial signage systems (BrightSign, Apple TV apps, Chromeboxes).
* **Why It Fails**: They require upfront hardware purchases ($200–$500 per screen) and monthly recurring software subscriptions for features you rarely need.

## Pro-Tips for Keeping Your TV Screen Awake During Long Events

To make sure your Smart TV display stays crisp and active throughout a full-day event, keep these quick tips in mind:

1. **Disable Auto Power-Off / Sleep Timers**: In your Smart TV settings, navigate to *General / System -> Eco Solution / Power Saving* and set *Auto Power Off* to **Off** or **8 Hours**.
2. **Disable Screen Savers for Web Browsers**: Ensure the TV browser's built-in sleep mode is disabled while viewing active WebSockets.
3. **Use Dark Mode for OLED & High-Contrast Views**: Dark backgrounds reduce power consumption on modern displays and prevent screen glare in brightly lit venues.
